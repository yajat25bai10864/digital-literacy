#Do’s
-Think before you post—consider how it may affect your reputation.
-Maintain privacy settings and protect personal information.
-Be respectful and polite in comments and messages.
-Share accurate and verified information only.
-Use social media for learning, networking, and positive engagement.
#Don’ts
-Don’t share sensitive personal details (address, phone number, etc.).
-Don’t engage in cyberbullying or offensive behavior.
-Don’t spread rumors, fake news, or unverified content.
-Don’t post inappropriate photos or content that may harm your image.
-Don’t waste excessive time scrolling—balance academics and social media.

#one situation where poor digital communication caused a real problem and what could have been done differently
-A common example of poor digital communication causing problems is when a student posts a sarcastic or offensive comment online without thinking. For instance, a student might joke about a sensitive topic in a group chat or on social media. However, others may misunderstand the tone and feel hurt or offended, leading to conflicts or even disciplinary action by the college. In some cases, such posts can damage the student’s reputation or relationships with peers and faculty.
-This situation could have been avoided if the student had thought carefully before posting and considered how the message might be interpreted by different audiences. Using respectful language, avoiding sensitive topics, and reviewing posts before sharing are simple but effective ways to communicate responsibly online.
