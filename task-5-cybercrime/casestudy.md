# Case Study: UPI / Online Payment Fraud

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) fraud is a common cybercrime in which attackers trick users into transferring money through digital payment apps. 
It usually begins when a fraudster contacts the victim via phone, SMS, or messaging apps, pretending to be a bank official, delivery agent, or customer support executive. 
The attacker then creates urgency by claiming there is a problem with the user’s account, a pending reward, or a failed transaction.
Next, the victim is asked to click on a malicious link or approve a “collect request” on their UPI app. 
In some cases, the fraudster may ask the victim to share an OTP (One-Time Password) or PIN, claiming it is needed to receive money. However, instead of receiving funds, 
the victim unknowingly authorizes a payment to the scammer. Once the transaction is completed, the money is quickly transferred to multiple accounts, making recovery difficult.
These scams often target students, elderly individuals, and people who are less aware of digital payment security. 
The consequences include financial loss, stress, and reduced trust in online systems.
Such incidents can be prevented by never sharing OTPs or PINs, verifying requests carefully, and understanding that receiving money does not require authorization.
